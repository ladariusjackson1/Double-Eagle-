Start with the honest version

Bookkeeping isn't the thing you're buying.

You're buying the answer to one question: was that deal, or that job, worth doing?

Nobody wakes up wanting transactions categorised. They want to stop guessing. The reason ordinary bookkeeping feels like an expense rather than an investment is that it produces a tidy record of the past and stops there — technically correct, strategically useless. You get a P&L that tells you the business made money in aggregate, which is exactly the level of detail at which you can't do anything about it.

For an agent, aggregate hides the fact that referral-sourced deals net you nearly double what portal leads do after cost. For a contractor, aggregate hides the fact that one category of work has been running at a loss all year while the rest carries it. The number you need isn't monthly. It's per unit of work.

Why ordinary bookkeeping isn't enough

Three gaps that a generalist can't close for you.

No cost allocation

A generic chart of accounts records that you spent $4,100 on materials. It cannot tell you which job consumed it, so job margin is unknowable no matter how clean the books are.

No forward view

Financial statements are a rear-view mirror. They tell you what happened in a month that's already closed, which is the wrong instrument for a business with lumpy commission or draw timing.

No interpretation

A report with no one to explain it gets opened once and filed. The value isn't the document — it's the half hour where someone who understands your business tells you what changed and why.

Our mechanism

Unit-level profitability, built on top of your books.

Three layers. The first is table stakes. The second and third are why people hire us instead of the cheapest bookkeeper who answers the phone.

A chart of accounts designed around your unit of work

For agents, that means gross commission income tracked against broker split, cap progress, E&O, transaction fees, referral fees and per-listing marketing — so the net on each deal is a number, not a feeling. For contractors, it means job phases, change orders, retainage and sub payments structured so costs attach to the job that caused them. This is set up once, during the Cleanup Sprint, and it's the thing that makes everything downstream possible.

Reconciled data run through our own cost-allocation tooling

Once your accounts are reconciled, we process them through internal tooling we built for exactly these two business types. It flags uncategorised and anomalous transactions for human review, allocates shared costs to the correct deal or job, and produces margin by category. A person reviews every exception — automation handles volume, judgement stays with us.

A monthly review where someone explains what it means

You get a KPI dashboard and a review call, not a PDF and silence. We tell you which work is carrying the business, which is dragging, where the cash gap is likely to land in the next 13 weeks, and what we'd change about pricing. That's the part that changes decisions.

Fit
