The problem

Your bank balance is not your profit.

Commission and draw schedules make the account look healthy in the weeks money lands and terrifying in the weeks it doesn't. Generic bookkeeping keeps the categories tidy but never answers the question you actually have.

Agents

Splits and fees eat the number you quoted yourself

Gross commission income is not what you keep. Broker split, cap, E&O, transaction fees, referral fees, team split, marketing on that listing — by the time it clears, the deal you called $12,000 was $6,400. Nobody itemises that back to you.

Contractors

The busy year that ended with nothing in the account

Revenue was up, crews were slammed, and December still felt tight. Without job costing you can't tell whether one bad bid ate three good jobs, or whether you're underpricing an entire category of work.

Both

March arrives as a surprise instead of a number

Books get reconstructed in a panic, deductions get missed because nobody tracked them in June, and the tax bill is whatever it turns out to be. You find out what last year cost you eleven months too late to change it.

What it costs you

Bad numbers aren't a bookkeeping problem. They're a pricing problem.

Every month without deal-level or job-level profit is a month of decisions made on a guess.
